### ecb_rates (+0.30, confidence 0.55)

A 25bp December hike to 2.75% is unanimous in the Reuters poll. Nagel calls further hikes 'very likely' and Lagarde says it would take 'significant changes' to deter a hike. Euro-area October flash HICP is 4.0% with energy inflation at 20.8%. The decision on 17 December falls inside the window, but it is largely priced, so the incremental hawkish move is modest.

Counter-argument: Reuters poll: 46 of 71 economists see 2.75% as the peak, and FXStreet notes economic uncertainty may halt tightening. A 'one and done' message on 17 December could push euro rates lower.

### fed_us_rates (+0.50, confidence 0.65)

A December 9 hike is nearly fully priced (CME FedWatch ~87% for 25bp, ~13% for 50bp), with January in play. Waller floats 50bp, Logan wants at least two more hikes, and Jefferson says inflation will drive decisions. US 2-year and 10-year yields are rising, with the 10-year above 5%, and MOVE is at the 89th percentile. CPI has beaten forecasts for three months and PPI is at a four-year high.

Counter-argument: The hike is already priced. Hammack and Paulson prefer gradual moves, so a 25bp hike with a gradual message could trigger a relief rally in rates. Yields already moved sharply this week.

### inflation (+0.50, confidence 0.60)

US core CPI has topped forecasts for three straight months and PPI is at a four-year high. Euro-area flash HICP is 4.0%, French HICP is 3.7% and Spanish CPI is 4.9%, and German wholesale prices are up 8.8% y/y. Central bankers describe pressures as broadening.

Counter-argument: Brent is about 20% below its late-October high and the IEA sees a Q4 oil surplus. Container freight is easing and 5y5y inflation expectations are stable at 2.17%. Energy-driven inflation could peak soon.

### growth_recession (+0.05, confidence 0.30)

Evidence is mixed. Euro-area Q3 GDP was +0.4% q/q (beat expectations) but slowing, with weak jobs and trade data. German growth forecasts were raised and GDPNow Q4 is 3.5%. Against that, US imports are slowing, copper is at four-month lows, and hikes are coming.

Counter-argument: Rising yields, aggressive Fed hikes and slowing container volumes point to a slowdown. The copper slide and the AI-linked equity wobble could signal weaker activity ahead.

### eurusd (-0.20, confidence 0.35)

EUR/USD is near 1.10, its weakest since May 2025, after three straight weekly losses. US yields are surging on Fed hike bets and the dollar index is near a 19-month high, so momentum favours the dollar.

Counter-argument: Euro speculative positioning is at an extreme net short (z -2.5, 1st percentile), a classic contrarian squeeze setup. A final ECB hike is also priced, and UBS targets 1.16 by December.

### oil (-0.15, confidence 0.30)

The IEA now sees a Q4 surplus, Saudi output is back near pre-war levels, OPEC+ production rose and US inventories built. Brent is down two straight weeks and about 20% below its late-October peak of ~$118. De-escalation hopes are weighing on prices.

Counter-argument: Iran/Houthi attacks and Hormuz risks can spike prices quickly. Speculative positioning is light (13th percentile), leaving room for short-covering, and Brent bounced on Nov 12.

### energy_weather (+0.10, confidence 0.30)

EU gas storage is ~71%, about 10 points below 2025 and 17 below the five-year average, and Germany is at its lowest for the date since 2011. Rystad says Europe enters winter with its lowest inventory of the 2020s. TTF is around €90-96 on Hormuz risk.

Counter-argument: The 14-day forecast is slightly warmer than normal for Europe, and TTF fell about 4% on the latest session. Rystad sees risks in both directions, and Hormuz flows are recovering.

### gold_silver (-0.20, confidence 0.35)

Gold has slipped below $4,000 to about $3,883, down about 4.6% on the week, and silver is down about 6%. Fed hikes and rising real yields are a headwind. Gold ETFs saw outflows in the week to Nov 6 and Metals Focus expects silver ETP outflows.

Counter-argument: Central bank buying is strong (Q3 demand up 42% y/y) and European ETFs are still taking inflows. Spec positioning is not stretched (z -0.3), so the dip could be bought.

### copper_metals (-0.05, confidence 0.20)

Copper has fallen for five straight weeks to four-month lows on the AI-equity selloff, soft industrial demand and a stronger dollar. LME stocks are rising. Goldman calls it positioning rather than fundamentals, with Chinese inventories at multi-year lows, so the signals roughly offset.

Counter-argument: Low Shanghai stocks, a record Yangshan premium and Goldman's positioning argument point to a rebound. The copper/gold ratio is high (87th percentile), which points to resilient growth.

### uranium (+0.05, confidence 0.20)

Counter-argument: Uranium equities are down about 45% over three months and spot is below term prices. Rising yields and the weak AI/nuclear-power equity theme could extend the downtrend.

### geopolitics_defence (+0.25, confidence 0.40)

Counter-argument: De-escalation after the midterms is being priced in, with Hormuz flows recovering. European defence stocks fell 1.4% on Nov 13, and the Commission warns of funding constraints after 2028.

### china_em (-0.15, confidence 0.30)

A rising dollar and US yields above 5% are headwinds for EM. The Shanghai Composite is down 3.5% over a month and sentiment is bearish despite state buying. Domestic demand is weak, the investment slump continues and Alibaba and Tencent sold off hard.

Counter-argument: China Q3 GDP accelerated to 4.5%, the trade surplus passed $1tn, and Korean exports to China are up 148%. Stimulus ahead of next month's policy meeting could lift sentiment.

### tech_ai_semis (+0.15, confidence 0.35)

Counter-argument: Huang's resignation from Nvidia triggered a selloff, Nvidia is down about 13% on the week, and its Nov 17 earnings may bring further weakness. Hedge funds are adding shorts and doubts about AI spending sustainability are growing.

### crypto (-0.20, confidence 0.35)

Bitcoin is down about 13% on the week to about $65k, below its 200-week line. Spot ETFs have seen seven straight days of outflows, stablecoin supply is shrinking (30-day growth at the 0th percentile) and longs have been liquidated. Rate-hike fears and the Nvidia shock weigh on sentiment.

Counter-argument: The Fear & Greed Index at 13 and heavy liquidations suggest capitulation, so a sharp mean-reversion bounce is plausible. Funding is neutral rather than crowded.

### risk_appetite (-0.10, confidence 0.30)

Counter-argument: US indices had their best week since August, equity fund inflows were the largest in 20 weeks, credit spreads are tightening, and Goldman raised its STOXX target. Momentum could carry equities into year-end.
